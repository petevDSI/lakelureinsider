On a map, Lake Lure and Chimney Rock look like a single stop. The drive is <Fact id="drive.lake-lure-to-chimney-rock.miles" /> and usually <Fact id="drive.lake-lure-to-chimney-rock.minutes" /> via <Fact id="drive.lake-lure-to-chimney-rock.route" />. That's short enough that most people stop thinking about it.

The catch is the road itself.

## What Is Going On With US 64/74A

Local news reports describe the work this way: <Fact id="chimney-rock.road.reconstruction-2029" />. A short drive is also a drive with very little slack in it. Any lane closure can turn a quick hop into a wait.

The road reopened to all traffic after the storm: <Fact id="road.us64.status" />. That's good news, but "open" and "free of delays" are different things.

## Where This Bites You

The place where this actually costs people is the **Chimney Rock entry deadline**. Entry closes at <Fact id="chimney-rock.hours.main.entry-close" /> in main season, and the late-afternoon visit we recommend in our [best-time guide](/insider-tips/best-time-to-visit-chimney-rock) depends on arriving near the end of the day. That's exactly when a construction delay turns a plan into a missed gate.

Two habits solve most of it:

- **Leave Lake Lure earlier than the map says you need to.** Treat the posted drive time as the best case, not the typical case, when you're aiming at a deadline.
- **Check DriveNC or the Town's updates before you go.** Conditions on a construction corridor can change from one day to the next.
