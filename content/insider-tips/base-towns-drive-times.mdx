Plenty of visitors book in a larger town nearby and drive in to Lake Lure. That can work well, but the three common choices are not equal.

These are driving estimates from a mapping source, checked in August 2026. They don't include the construction delays we describe in our [drive guide](/insider-tips/hickory-nut-gorge-drive-buffer), so allow extra.

## Hendersonville

<Fact id="drive.lake-lure-to-hendersonville.miles" />, about <Fact id="drive.lake-lure-to-hendersonville.minutes" />, via US 64 W.

The closest of the three. And US 64 here is Chimney Rock Road, which is where the apple orchards are. If you're staying in Hendersonville in the fall, our guide to the [orchards on Chimney Rock Road](/insider-tips/apple-orchards-on-chimney-rock-road) is a drive you'll already be making.

## Black Mountain

<Fact id="drive.lake-lure-to-black-mountain.miles" />, about <Fact id="drive.lake-lure-to-black-mountain.minutes" />, via NC-9 N.

A different approach, coming down NC-9 from the north. It's a reasonable base if your trip also takes in the Black Mountain area, but it runs a few minutes longer than Hendersonville if Lake Lure is the main event.

## Asheville

<Fact id="drive.lake-lure-to-asheville.miles" />, about <Fact id="drive.asheville-to-lake-lure.minutes" />, via US 74A W.

The farthest in time. Asheville has by far the most lodging and dining, which is the reason to choose it, but you'll feel the drive at both ends of a day, especially if you're aiming for Chimney Rock's entry cutoff.

## Which to Choose

- **Lake Lure is the whole trip:** stay in Lake Lure or as close as you can. See our [where to stay](/where-to-stay) page.
- **Lake Lure is one stop of several:** Asheville or Black Mountain can make sense, with Lake Lure as a day trip.
- **You want the shortest commute:** Hendersonville.

<InsiderTip>
The drive times are for the trip in. Plan your return around daylight as well, since mountain roads and an active construction corridor are easier to handle in the light.
</InsiderTip>
