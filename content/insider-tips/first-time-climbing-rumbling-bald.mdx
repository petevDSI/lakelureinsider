Rumbling Bald is a free-access climbing area inside Chimney Rock State Park, with an estimated 1,500 bouldering problems plus trad and sport routes. It's widely described as one of the most beginner-friendly major crags in western North Carolina, and it's one of the better outdoor activities in the area that doesn't cost a ticket.

If this is your first visit, a few facts decide how it goes.

## The Permit

Access is free and self-guided, but there's one required step: fill out a climbing permit at the kiosk in the parking lot. Only the south side is currently open to climbing, so expect your options to be narrower than older guides suggest.

The dedicated climbers' lot is on Boys Camp Road. There is no camping at the lot or at the crags.

## The Season

Fall and winter are the popular season. In summer the south-facing rock gets hot, and the area can be overgrown with poison ivy. If you're reading this in the autumn, you're in the right window.

## Guide or No Guide?

If you've never climbed outdoors, a guided day is the sensible start. Appalachian Climbing School is based in Lake Lure and guides at Rumbling Bald, with half-day intro top-rope sessions, full-day sport and lead days, and guided bouldering. Technical gear, crash pads, and park access are included, and no experience is required for the intro programs. Its prices aren't published, so contact the school for rates.

Climbing carries real risk, and a first day with someone who can judge conditions and check your setup is a good use of money.

<InsiderTip>
Guided climbing on the Chimney Rock monolith itself, which runs through Fox Mountain Guides when it's available, was on hold when we last checked. Rumbling Bald is the open alternative, so don't plan around the monolith without checking.
</InsiderTip>

## Getting There

Boys Camp Road leads to the climbers' lot, and a bridge project on that road had <Fact id="construction.boys-camp-bridge.status" /> when we last checked in early August. Confirm current conditions before you drive out.
