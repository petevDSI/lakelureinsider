On election night, Lake Lure knew who won but not how the mayor's challengers did. All 286 write-in votes for mayor were reported as a single total until election officials sorted the write-ins by name.

Now the full count is in. Kathy Tanner, who launched her write-in campaign on Sept. 10, less than eight weeks before Election Day, received 253 votes, about 35% of the total. John Edmonds Kozma, the other write-in candidate, received 24. Mayor Carol Pritchett, the only name on the printed ballot, won re-election with 434 votes, about 60%.

The new count also settled the one tie on the ballot. Friedrich Jaeger-Faber and Richard Sayles finished Tuesday night at 253 votes each for third place in the Town Council race. With one provisional ballot added, Jaeger-Faber finished ahead, 254 to 253. The tie never affected who won: Commissioner Jim Proctor and Dave Lusk took the two seats.

## The final count

**Mayor**

- Carol Pritchett: 434 (60.19%) — *elected*
- Kathy Tanner (write-in): 253 (35.09%)
- John Kozma (write-in): 24 (3.33%)
- Other write-in names: 10 (1.39%)

**Town Council (two seats)**

- Jim Proctor: 431 (32.26%) — *elected*
- Dave Lusk: 384 (28.74%) — *elected*
- Friedrich Jaeger-Faber: 254 (19.01%)
- Richard Sayles: 253 (18.94%)
- Write-in names: 14 (1.05%)

Compared with election night, the mayor's race gained one vote, a provisional ballot cast for Kozma, for 721 in all. In the council race, one provisional ballot added a vote each for Lusk and Jaeger-Faber.

Every ballot has now been counted. The results become official when Rutherford County certifies them at its canvass, scheduled for 11 a.m. Friday, Nov. 13.

## A write-in showing worth noting

Running as a write-in is hard. A voter has to remember the name, find the blank line and spell it out by hand. Tanner and Kozma both started after candidate filing closed on July 17, with Pritchett the only person to file ([our September report](/news/write-in-mayor-candidates-2026)). Rutherford County's elections director confirmed to Lake Lure Insider that write-in votes in a municipal race count with no advance paperwork ([county confirms](/news/write-in-rules-county-confirms)).

Tanner still won more than a third of the vote. Pritchett's margin over her was 181 votes.

- **Early voting** made up the largest share of ballots, 414 of 721. Pritchett won it 264 to 129.
- **Election Day** was much closer. Of 272 votes, Pritchett received 147 and Tanner 116, about 54% to 43%.
- **Mail ballots** were few, 34 in all, and Pritchett took 23 of them.

The numbers show where Tanner was strongest. They can't show why, and Lake Lure Insider won't guess.

In a Facebook post after the election, Tanner congratulated Pritchett and said she looks forward to continuing to serve the community "for many years to come." Kozma, in a video, thanked his supporters and said the town's dam and sewer problems remain. ## The council race

Proctor, a former mayor, returns for another four-year term with 431 votes. Lusk, a commercial contractor and volunteer firefighter, joins the council with 384.

The race for third place came down to how people voted. Sayles led Jaeger-Faber among early voters, 134 to 122. Jaeger-Faber led on Election Day, 115 to 110, and among mail ballots, 16 to 9, and he received one of the race's two provisional votes. Both candidates congratulated Proctor and Lusk the morning after the election.

## What happens next

- **County canvass:** 11 a.m. Friday, Nov. 13. The Rutherford County Board of Elections certifies the results. The meeting is open to the public.
- **Swearing-in:** The new council takes its oath by the Town Council's first regular December meeting, currently scheduled for Dec. 8.
