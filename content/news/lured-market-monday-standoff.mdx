**The Town's settlement offer — posted Friday night with a Monday deadline — expires today without an agreement. Over the weekend, Lured Market & Grill publicly rejected it as "an ultimatum," Commissioner Jim Proctor publicly called the Market's counter-ask unreasonable, and Second Mountain says a 2023 Town document central to the boathouse dispute has disappeared from the Town's own website. Here's what each side said, in their own words, and what today's deadline actually means.**

This is a follow-up to our August 29 report, <a href="/news/lured-market-settlement-offer-compromise" className="font-semibold text-(--lake) underline">The Town Calls It a Compromise. Here's What Each Side Actually Gives Up.</a> For every document and on-record statement behind this story, see our <a href="/news/lured-market-source-documents" className="font-semibold text-(--lake) underline">running source-document index</a>.

## Where Friday left off

As we reported Friday, the Town posted a settlement offer at 7:55 p.m. on August 28: Second Mountain permanently abandons any claim to the police boathouse, pays $3,218.00 in past-due rent, and continues under the existing lease at the existing rent — with no adjustment for what it's giving up and no revised lease terms. The deadline was Monday, August 31 — today.

## Lured Market's answer: not a rejection, a counteroffer

Lured Market & Grill posted its full response Saturday night, August 29, at 8:25 p.m. The core of it: Second Mountain will still give up the boathouse and the related concession arrangement for non-motorized rentals, but says the rent has to be adjusted to reflect what's being removed from the deal, and the lease needs to be clarified going forward. In the business's own words: "It is absolutely reasonable for the rent to be adjusted accordingly." The post says Second Mountain submitted a written proposal the Thursday after Wednesday's council meeting, offered "a few different rates and structures," and asked the Town to counter or edit the terms — and says it received no response before Friday's take-it-or-leave-it offer arrived instead.

The post also addresses a quote the Town's own Friday statement used. The Town's release quoted Paul Brock saying, "I'm happy to let the boathouse go, and we'll do it. But we need to stop this litigation." Lured Market says the video of Wednesday's meeting shows a line immediately before that one, which the Town's version omits: "We're not getting something that we think we bargained for." The business's read: Paul offered to give up the boathouse as part of a fair resolution, not as a surrender on every other term while facing immediate eviction. We have not independently reviewed the video ourselves to confirm the sequencing, and we note this remains a description from one side rather than a document as of this writing.

On timing, the post gives a more precise account than we had Friday: the Town's attorney emailed the proposal to Second Mountain's attorney at 6:12 p.m.; their attorney replied at 6:20 p.m. that he hadn't had time to review it and would forward it along; the Town posted the offer publicly at 7:55 p.m. — about an hour and 43 minutes after first sending it — with the Monday deadline attached.

## A 2023 document that's no longer where it used to be

The most significant new claim in the weekend's posts has nothing to do with rent. Lured Market says the September 6, 2023 Town Council meeting packet — the one establishing that a previous prospective tenant's proposal included using the Town's covered boathouse slips, and that boathouse and dock access were part of the property's use going back to before Second Mountain existed as a bidder — is no longer retrievable from the Town's website. In the business's words: "The meeting webpage remains online, but the packet containing this information has recently been made unavailable there. Fortunately, we have documented every bit of this, and a complete copy was preserved in Lured email records. We do not know why the packet is unavailable, and we will not speculate." The business posted its own copy: [the September 6, 2023 packet, via Google Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1PX3Q3aR732FvxoBTcK__tuN_mHkZM2RZ/view?usp=sharing).

We're not speculating either about why it's missing — we don't know, and neither, on its own account, does Second Mountain. But readers don't have to take Second Mountain's word for what the document says: this outlet already cited the same September 6, 2023 packet directly, with verbatim quotes, in our August 29 report — two days before Second Mountain's post — drawing on a copy supplied directly to this outlet. The document's content isn't new to our reporting; what's new is the claim that it's no longer accessible on the Town's own site. We're checking the Town's meeting-packet page ourselves and will update this piece with what we find, and we've asked Pete to confirm the original source of our own copy so we can be precise about the chain here rather than imply more independence than we can back up.

## Commissioner Proctor: "Paul Brock wants more"

Hours before Lured Market posted its response — at 2:02 p.m. Saturday, in the Lake Lure Local Facebook group — Commissioner Jim Proctor gave the clearest public Town-aligned account yet of why he thinks the weekend's back-and-forth is happening. In his telling, Paul Brock agreed to the terms Proctor presented at Wednesday's meeting, then "once again, changed his demands." Proctor specifically disputes the idea that Second Mountain can't afford rent on both Suite A (the Market) and Suite B (the gym space Lake Lure Gym is vacating): "we all heard at the same meeting, from folks that want to take over the gym lease, so that is not an issue," he wrote. He put a number on Second Mountain's ask for the first time publicly — a reduction he says "would cost the town more than $100,000.00 over the course of the lease" — and called it "unreasonable, especially considering there were multiple entities originally interested in renting the building." His stated bottom line: "Paul and the Market have a choice: Stop the lawsuit they brought and abide by the current lease, or a new lease is created with a reduction in rent with an upset bid period." An upset bid period would mean putting the space out for other offers before finalizing any reduced rate with the current tenant.

We haven't independently verified Proctor's $100,000 figure or confirmed the identity of the "folks that want to take over the gym lease" — both are his characterization, not something this outlet has checked against a document. We're also noting, as we have before, that Proctor continues to frame these as personal remarks rather than an official Town position, even though he sits on the Council.

The two accounts conflict directly on one factual point: whether the Suite B rent exposure is a real problem (Lured Market's Friday post, and testimony at Wednesday's meeting from Suite B-adjacent sources previously reported in our coverage) or a non-issue because replacement tenants are lined up (Proctor's account). We don't have enough to resolve that conflict as of this writing, and we're not going to pretend otherwise.

## What today's deadline actually means

The Town's offer required payment and a signed release by today, August 31. Based on Lured Market's own Saturday post, that isn't happening on the Town's exact terms — the business is countering, not accepting. Lured Market's post says it "fully expect[s] that it still plans to evict us next week," which would track with a previously reported (but not yet court-confirmed) September 3 date connected to the eviction matter. We have not confirmed a hearing date against the actual court docket, and we're not going to print one as fact until we have.

What we're watching for today: whether the Town responds publicly or privately to Second Mountain's counteroffer, whether the Town moves forward with eviction proceedings now that its Monday deadline has passed unmet, and whether the missing 2023 packet reappears — or whether the Town explains why it isn't there. We'll update this piece, or file a new one, as any of that develops.

## What we're not saying

We're not asserting the Town removed the September 6, 2023 packet deliberately, or that its absence is anything other than what it appears to be until we or the Town can explain it. We're not verifying Commissioner Proctor's $100,000 figure or his claim about interested replacement tenants for Suite B — those are his statements, not confirmed facts. We're not verifying Lured Market's account of the video sequencing around the "bargained for" line — that's their description, not something we've reviewed ourselves. And we're not predicting what happens today; we're telling you what each side has said and what we're watching for.
