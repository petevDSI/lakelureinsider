**Monday night's public hearing was scheduled to be about the Town's comprehensive plan. It became, instead, the largest and tensest Lake Lure town meeting in recent memory — a crowd well beyond the usual turnout, extra police and fire personnel on hand, a mayor who tried to keep public comment on Lured Market off the agenda entirely, and a business owner who used his three minutes to call for new town leadership. Not much was decided. A lot was said.**

This is a follow-up to our August 24 report, <a href="/news/lured-market-lease-council-approved" className="font-semibold text-(--lake) underline">The Lease the Council Actually Approved</a>, and our August 23 report, <a href="/news/lured-market-town-lease-fight" className="font-semibold text-(--lake) underline">Inside the Fight Over Lake Lure's Best-Known Business</a>.

## A Hearing About the Comprehensive Plan, Until It Wasn't

Monday's meeting was formally a public hearing on the Town's comprehensive plan — not the Lured Market dispute. Before it started, the mayor moved to keep comment on the Lured Market situation off that agenda, directing residents instead to the Town's regular Wednesday session.

It didn't hold. The room was packed well past what a routine planning hearing draws, and Fox Carolina — the only outside outlet to cover the meeting so far — described supporters as having "packed a public hearing Monday night to voice concerns."

Lake Lure Insider was in the room. Extra police and fire personnel were visibly on hand, more than is typical for a planning hearing, and moved closer to the front as the first speaker, Hilary Yoxall, began talking about Lured Market over the mayor's attempt to stop her. She didn't stop. She said she intended to be heard, and she was.

## Hilary Yoxall, Grace Foods, and the Hub That Wasn't the Town's

Yoxall is the founder and director of Grace Foods Co-Op, a real, currently operating food-relief organization — that much Lake Lure Insider confirmed independently at [gracefoodsnc.org](https://www.gracefoodsnc.org/about). According to Yoxall and to what Lake Lure Insider has been told locally, Grace Foods took root at Lured Market & Grill in the aftermath of Hurricane Helene, when the market became a hub where volunteers fed and supplied a neighborhood that had lost power, water, and road access.

Fox Carolina's reporting backs up the shape of that account, even without naming Grace Foods. Resident Carol Pennell told the outlet:

> "They used their place as the hub to serve free food to people in the neighborhood. It's emotional. Why pick on the heart of the comeback of Lake Lure?"

Restaurant co-owner Cara Brock made a related point about the comprehensive plan the hearing was nominally about:

> "When we talk about rebuilding and a comprehensive plan for making Lake Lure great again, taking away one of the only businesses that has survived and is even open in that area, it just doesn't make any sense. It's devastating. Some kind of compromise has to happen."

One more thing belongs in this section, stated plainly as Lake Lure Insider's own observation rather than a documented fact: through the months of Helene recovery, we did not see the mayor visit the hub at Lured Market — the site where a meaningful share of the town's storm relief was actually happening. We can't prove a negative, and we welcome a correction from the Town if that's wrong.

## Paul Brock: "We Need New Leadership"

Paul Brock, Lured Market's co-owner, spoke to thank the supporters who turned out — a much larger crowd, he noted, than the business had seen at any prior meeting — and urged them to come back for the Town's regular Wednesday session, where public comment on the dispute is actually on the agenda. He also used part of his time to call for new leadership on the Town Council.

That call lands in a real election cycle. One seat on the five-member Council (Mayor Carol Pritchett, Mayor Pro Tem David DiOrio, and Commissioners Patrick Bryant, Jim Proctor, and Scott Doster) will turn over as its current holder steps down. Commissioner Jim Proctor — a name regular readers of this site will recognize from our August 24 report, where several of his own public statements about this case didn't hold up against the Town's own documents — is up for election this cycle.

**For the record:** after our August 24 report was shared in a local Facebook group, Proctor responded there directly, pointing to an early comment in that thread ("Lake Lure Insider is not a neutral bystander in this story") as, in his words, saying "it all." He's right that we're not neutral — we've said so in every one of these pieces, including this one. We stand by the reporting itself, which was checked against the Town's own primary-source documents, not against our opinion of the outcome.

## What Does the Town Want With the Old ABC Building?

The Town says it has no plans for the Lured Market building — the former ABC store the market currently occupies. Lake Lure Insider has not seen a document that contradicts that statement.

But there's a plausible reason the Town might want the building back, and it's sitting in the Town's own public materials: Town Hall and the Police Department were flooded out of their original building during Hurricane Helene and have been operating since mid-2025 out of leased space at The Landings, on Buffalo Creek Road. The Town's own announcement of that move describes it as "an interim space until more permanent decisions are made regarding the Town Hall facility," and an August 2025 Council meeting summary quotes Mayor Pritchett describing a "commercial lease" that will hold staff "until plans for a permanent Town Hall can be solidified." Neither document says the old ABC store is the intended solution — but a Town actively looking for a permanent home for its displaced Town Hall, holding the deed to a vacant-if-it-evicts building two blocks from where its staff already works, is a plausible enough theory that it deserves to be said out loud, clearly labeled as a theory and not a fact.

**What Lake Lure Insider could not find:** a public dollar figure for what the Town is paying to lease space at The Landings. Neither the Town's website nor its published meeting summaries disclose the rent, and it does not appear to have come up in a way that made it into public minutes. If a taxpayer wants to know what the interim arrangement is costing the town, that appears to require a direct public records request — the Town's own General Statute obligation to produce records is the same one at issue in the Lured Market lawsuit itself.

## Wednesday, 8:30 a.m. — Not Wednesday Night

Here's a discrepancy worth flagging directly, because getting it wrong means someone shows up at the wrong time. Fox Carolina reported that the mayor "welcomes community members to speak on the matter during the town's regular meeting on Wednesday night." But the Town's own posted meeting calendar lists Wednesday, August 26 as a Work Session and Action Meeting starting at **8:30 a.m.** — not an evening meeting. The Town's next evening meeting on its calendar is a regular Council meeting on September 8 at 5:00 p.m.

Paul Brock told Monday's crowd to come back Wednesday morning. The Town's own calendar agrees with him, not with Fox Carolina's paraphrase. Lake Lure Insider could not confirm from the calendar listing alone whether public comment is formally on Wednesday's work-session agenda — readers planning to attend should check the Town's posted agenda closer to the date.

## The Petition, By the Numbers

As of this morning, the petition asking the Town to reinstate Lured Market's lease and put the decision to an open Council vote has **224 signatures**. Of those, **108 — 48% — listed Lake Lure as their city.**

Measured against the Town's own 2020 Census population of 1,365, those 108 Lake Lure signers work out to roughly **1 in 13 town residents, or about 8% of the entire population** — children and all. That's a floor, not a ceiling: the Census figure counts everyone, including people too young to sign anything, and the city field on the petition is self-reported and unverified against actual town limits, so it may include some signers who live in the surrounding Lake Lure area but outside the Town's boundary, and may miss others who typed something else. Treat 8% as a rough, conservative read on how much of the town has weighed in through this one channel — not a precise vote count.

## Sourcing Note

The account of Monday's hearing — the attempt to keep Lured Market off the agenda, the size of the crowd relative to past meetings, the presence of extra police and fire personnel, Hilary Yoxall being interrupted and continuing to speak, and Paul Brock's remarks including his call for new leadership — is Lake Lure Insider's own eyewitness account; our founder attended the meeting in person. The observation that the mayor was not seen at the Lured Market relief hub during Helene recovery is likewise our own observation, not a documented fact, and is stated as such.

Fox Carolina's August 25 report independently corroborates that the hearing was packed and quotes residents Cara Brock and Carol Pennell; those quotations are reproduced here from that report and were not independently re-confirmed with the speakers by Lake Lure Insider. Fox Carolina's characterization of Wednesday's meeting as occurring "Wednesday night" conflicts with the Town's own posted calendar, which is treated here as the more authoritative source since it is the Town's primary record of its own schedule.

Hilary Yoxall's role as founder and director of Grace Foods Co-Op was independently confirmed against the organization's own website. The specific claim that Grace Foods began operating out of Lured Market during Helene recovery is reported here as Yoxall's and the community's account; Lake Lure Insider did not locate an independent, dated record establishing exactly when or where Grace Foods began operating.

The Town Council roster (Mayor Carol Pritchett, Mayor Pro Tem David DiOrio, Commissioners Patrick Bryant, Jim Proctor, and Scott Doster) and the Landings relocation details are drawn from the Town's own published pages. The Town has not disclosed the rent it pays at The Landings in any public document Lake Lure Insider located. The Lake Lure population figure (1,365) is the Town's own cited figure from the 2020 Census. Petition figures are a snapshot as of this article's publication and will change.

Jim Proctor's Facebook comment responding to our August 24 report is quoted from a public comment on a public Facebook group post, under his own name.
