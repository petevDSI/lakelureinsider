LAKE LURE, N.C. — The insurance pool that covers the Town of Lake Lure's buildings has sued the Town.

On Sept. 17, the Interlocal Risk Financing Fund of North Carolina filed suit in Wake County Superior Court against Lake Lure and Sedgwick Claims Management Services, the company the fund hired to adjust Hurricane Helene claims. The fund is run by the N.C. League of Municipalities, the statewide association Lake Lure belongs to. The case is *Interlocal Risk Financing Fund of North Carolina v. Sedgwick Claims Management Services, Inc. and the Town of Lake Lure*, [No. 26CV035241-910](https://portal-nc.tylertech.cloud/Portal/Home/Dashboard/29).

The complaint itself is sealed. But the court file lists the claims as breach of contract, indemnification and "money owed." The fund has demanded a jury trial against both defendants.

The Town has not announced the lawsuit. But it has discussed it behind closed doors. On Sept. 23, six days after the suit was filed, Council went into closed session "in regards to Second Mountain LLC and Interlocal Risk Financing Fund, NC," according to the [draft minutes of that meeting](https://www.townoflakelure.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/town_council/meeting/8842/10-13-2026_town_council_regular_meeting_packet.pdf#page=39) published in the Oct. 13 Council packet. Council's [Oct. 13 agenda](https://www.townoflakelure.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/town_council/meeting/8842/10-13-2026_town_council_regular_meeting_packet.pdf#page=2) also ended with a closed session "for Attorney Client Privilege or Legal Claims."

**Why it matters:** the exhibits filed with the complaint point to the insurance money Lake Lure collected after Helene. The Town's own records show that money is already in dispute, and the Town is counting on it to rebuild its flooded Town Hall.

## Who's who

**The N.C. League of Municipalities (NCLM).** Founded in 1908 as the Carolina Municipal Association, it is the statewide membership group for North Carolina cities and towns. It lobbies the General Assembly, trains local officials and runs self-funded insurance programs for its members: workers' compensation, employee health benefits, and property and liability coverage. It administers the fund that filed this suit. ([About the League](https://prodweb.nclm.org/about-the-league/))

**The Interlocal Risk Financing Fund of North Carolina (IRFFNC).** Also called the Property and Casualty Trust. It was formed in the mid-1980s, when commercial insurers were pulling back from local governments. It is not an insurance company. It is a pool: member towns pay premiums into a shared fund, and the fund pays covered losses. A board of trustees oversees it; League staff handle underwriting and claims. Lake Lure joined in July 2005. After Helene, the League reported more than 300 claims statewide, about $45 million in covered exposures. ([Property & Casualty Trust](https://www.nclm.org/insurance-risk-management/property-casualty-trust/) · [NCLM 2024 annual report](https://www.nclm.org/media/rj1bjnsj/2024annualreport-85x11.pdf))

**Sedgwick Claims Management Services, Inc.** A Memphis-based claims-handling and adjusting company, among the largest in the world. Insurers and self-insured groups hire it to inspect damage, value losses and recommend payments. The private-equity firm Carlyle has been majority owner since a [$6.7 billion deal in 2018](https://www.businessinsurance.com/private-equity-firm-carlyle-group-buys-majority-stake-in-sedgwick-claims-managem/); a [2024 investment](https://www.altas.com/news/sedgwick-announces-strategic-partnership-with-altas-partners) valued it at about $13.2 billion, with more than 33,000 employees in 80 countries. Its CEO is [Mike Arbour](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/news/breaking-news/sedgwick-reveals-new-ceo-amid-leadership-promotions-230596.aspx). The fund hired Sedgwick for catastrophe adjusting in North Carolina.

**The Town of Lake Lure.** The insured member. Its finance staff and its FEMA-and-insurance consultant, ICF, handled the Helene claims. Then–Finance Director Stephen Ford led the insurance work. The Town is now recruiting a new finance director, and Town Manager Olivia Stewman has served as interim finance officer since Sept. 23.

## What Lake Lure's policy covers, and what it doesn't

The fund made three exhibits public, and they read like an outline of its argument. Two clauses stand out.

**The dam is not covered property.** The fund's [property form](/documents/irffnc-v-lake-lure-exhibit-d-property-coverage-form.pdf#page=4) (version PR-01, July 2024) lists what it does not insure. Near the top: "Dams, levees or dikes and the related structures that regulate or direct the flow of water, including but not limited to the drain valve, intake area, spillway conduit, penstock and tunnel, flues, and grills." That is close to a parts list for Lake Lure's dam and its hydroelectric intake. The same page excludes most bridges, roadways, and piers and docks not listed on the Town's property schedule.

**Flood coverage stops at the floodplain line.** Ordinary property coverage excludes flood. Lake Lure carried a [flood endorsement](/documents/irffnc-v-lake-lure-exhibit-e-flood-endorsement.pdf#page=2), but it is narrow:

- **Limit:** $5,000,000 per flood and per year. That matches the "up to $5,000,000" in insurance the Town told residents to expect in [December 2024](https://www.townoflakelure.com/community/page/lake-lure-storm-recovery-updates-121224).
- **Deductible:** $50,000 per flood.
- **Where it applies:** only in FEMA flood zones B, C and X. The endorsement says the fund "will not pay for loss or damage to any covered property... located in any location either wholly or partially in flood zones A, V, or a one hundred (100) year Flood Plain." ([Exhibit E, p. 3](/documents/irffnc-v-lake-lure-exhibit-e-flood-endorsement.pdf#page=3))
- **Also excluded:** boathouses and other structures over water.

**Where is Town Hall?** Lake Lure's Town Hall at 2948 Memorial Highway was flooded in Helene. Lake Lure Insider checked the address against FEMA's [National Flood Hazard Layer](https://hazards.fema.gov/arcgis/rest/services/public/NFHL/MapServer), which places it in **Zone AE**, the 100-year floodplain. If that holds for the map in effect in September 2024, Town Hall would have had no flood coverage under this endorsement. We are asking the Town to confirm it. These forms are also not the whole policy: coverage applies only to property on the Town's schedules, and other endorsements could change the picture.

**If the fund pays, it can collect.** Members must cooperate with the fund and its contractors. If the fund pays a loss, it takes over the member's right to recover from anyone responsible, and any recovery or salvage goes to the fund "until the sum paid by IRFFNC has been recovered." ([Exhibit D, p. 11](/documents/irffnc-v-lake-lure-exhibit-d-property-coverage-form.pdf#page=11))

## Why the case is sealed, and for how long

The complaint is sealed to protect Sedgwick's business information, not the Town's. And the fund itself says it shouldn't stay that way.

The fund filed under Rule 27 of North Carolina's General Rules of Practice, which lets a filer submit documents "provisionally under seal" while the court decides. In its [motion to seal](/documents/irffnc-v-lake-lure-motion-to-seal-2026-09-17.pdf), the fund said the complaint and exhibits do not contain its own confidential or proprietary business information, and that it "does not believe they contain any information that justifies the documents remaining under seal indefinitely." It sealed them only because Sedgwick "may believe" they contain Sedgwick's confidential business information, chiefly its adjusting contract and reports. Until a judge rules, only the lawyers can see them.

**The clock.** Under [Rule 27](https://ruledex.com/north-carolina/general-rules-of-practice-for-the-superior-and-district-courts/rule-27-sealed-documents-and-protective-orders/), anyone served with the motion has 20 days to file a brief supporting or opposing sealing; Sedgwick was served Sept. 28. A hearing follows. If no one justifies sealing, the court "may order that the document... be made public." As of Oct. 13, no order had been entered.

**The legal standard.** North Carolina's constitution guarantees open courts. Under the state Supreme Court's 1999 *Virmani* decision, a judge may seal court records only for a compelling interest, after considering alternatives, and with written findings. A contractor's pricing might qualify. A dispute over how public money was paid out generally would not. ([RCFP Open Courts Compendium, N.C.](https://www.rcfp.org/open-courts-compendium/north-carolina/))

**The public can ask.** Under G.S. 1-72.1, any person, including a news outlet, may file a limited motion asserting a right of access to sealed court records without becoming a party to the case.

## Has the League been here before?

We found no record of the League investigating, auditing or suing Lake Lure before this case. Lawsuits by the fund against its own members appear rare; the one appellate case we found, from 2007, was a coverage fight over a police officer, not a suit against a town ([*Interlocal Risk Financing Fund v. Ryals*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1386964/interlocal-risk-financing-fund-of-north-carolina-v/)).

What the record does show is a close working relationship, which makes the lawsuit more striking:

- **Oct. 14, 2024:** League insurance staff came to [inspect Helene damage](https://www.townoflakelure.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/town_council/page/8314/10-14-24.pdf).
- **Nov. 20, 2024:** the finance director told Council the Town had ["significant response from the League."](https://mccmeetings.blob.core.usgovcloudapi.net/lakelurenc-pubu/MEET-Minutes-7accab03d13d470e9b107e12079268ff.pdf)
- **Sept. 9, 2025:** Council approved a [disaster-recovery agreement](https://mccmeetings.blob.core.usgovcloudapi.net/lakelurenc-pubu/MEET-Packet-25665b9028e2499f9385277633d234a2.pdf#page=82) under which the League, using state Helene money, provides the Town legal help through the Raleigh firm Parker Poe.
- **Sept. 23, 2026**, six days after the suit was filed: Council met in closed session about the fund's case, and in open session was told the League "provides significant insurance coverage and risk management services to the Town," and that personnel policies meeting League minimum standards are ["important for continued eligibility and compliance."](https://www.townoflakelure.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/town_council/meeting/packets/8853/09-23-2026_town_council_work_session_packet.pdf#page=40) The new policy was on Council's [Oct. 13 consent agenda](https://www.townoflakelure.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/town_council/meeting/8842/10-13-2026_town_council_regular_meeting_packet.pdf#page=2).

The League is now, at once, the Town's insurer, its disaster-recovery legal provider and its opponent in court.

## What could be happening (analysis)

*This section is analysis. The complaint is sealed, and none of these explanations has been confirmed.*

The likeliest explanation is that the fund believes it paid Lake Lure for losses its policy didn't cover, and wants that money back from the Town and from Sedgwick, whose adjusting led to the payments. The claim types fit that reading: "money owed" points at the Town, while "contract" and "indemnification" point at Sedgwick's adjusting agreement. Three versions are plausible, and they are not mutually exclusive.

**1. Town Hall and the floodplain.** Sedgwick adjusts the Town's losses; the fund pays an early installment, including what the Town called $1.3 million for Town Hall. The fund then concludes Town Hall sits in Zone AE, where its flood endorsement pays nothing. *For:* the floodplain clause is in a public exhibit, and the Town's own minutes now call the Town Hall money "contested." *Against:* no public exhibit mentions Town Hall by name.

**2. The dam.** Sedgwick's reports may have valued damage to dam structures the policy expressly excludes: the drain valve, intake, spillway conduit and penstock. On Feb. 4, 2025, a week after Sedgwick's final report, the League and Sedgwick were emailing about "looking at the dam." Damage to the drain-valve project may also have been covered by the contractor's own insurance. *For:* two sealed exhibits concern the dam, and the exclusion reads like a description of it. *Against:* we have no record that the fund paid anything on the dam.

**3. A fight mainly with the adjuster.** The core dispute may be the fund against Sedgwick over how it handled catastrophe claims, with the Town named because it received the disputed money. Exhibits about a "claims list" and "updated directives" suggest an argument over what Sedgwick was told to adjust. *For:* Sedgwick is the lead defendant, and sealing turns on its position. *Against:* if only one member town was sued, Lake Lure's own claims are likely at the center.

**What we considered and set aside:** a defense of the Town against outside lawsuits (the docket lists no declaratory-judgment claim, and the exhibits are about adjusting the Town's own losses), and unpaid premiums (that dispute would not need Sedgwick or the dam).

**Insurance and FEMA are linked.** FEMA must subtract any insurance money a town receives for a building ([FEMA, duplication of benefits](https://www.fema.gov/appeal/duplication-benefits-5)). Its rules also generally reduce aid for an uninsured building in a mapped floodplain by what a federal flood policy would have paid. So a fight over the Town Hall insurance money could change what FEMA will pay, in either direction.

**The General Fund has little slack.** On Oct. 13, Council was asked to approve using [$433,717 of fund balance](https://www.townoflakelure.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/town_council/meeting/8842/10-13-2026_town_council_regular_meeting_packet.pdf#page=136) for a fire-truck down payment. The Town's insurance line for this fiscal year already shows [$257,021 paid against $210,000 budgeted](https://www.townoflakelure.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/town_council/meeting/8842/10-13-2026_town_council_regular_meeting_packet.pdf#page=22). Any repayment to the fund, plus the Town's own legal fees, would come from the same place.

**Residents feel it four ways:**

1. **Taxes or delays:** money repaid to the fund is money not spent on recovery, or that must be raised another way.
2. **Premiums:** pool losses are shared. Disputed payouts can push up what every member town pays, Lake Lure included.
3. **Transparency:** the Town announced its insurance money in 2025 but has said nothing publicly about the lawsuit.
4. **Audits:** the Town's audit for the year ended June 30, 2025, which would show these receipts, is not done. A repayment demand is the kind of liability auditors must report.

## Did the Town follow protocol when it joined?

Yes, as far as the public record shows, and joining a League pool is what most North Carolina towns do.

**The 2005 paperwork looks routine.** [Exhibit C](/documents/irffnc-v-lake-lure-exhibit-c-2005-resolution-interlocal-agreement.pdf) is Resolution No. 05-07-12C, adopted by the Lake Lure Town Council on July 12, 2005. It is signed by then-Mayor Jim Proctor, now a commissioner on the November ballot, attested by Town Clerk Mary A. Flack, and bears the Town seal. It approves the standard "Interlocal Agreement for a Group Self-Insurance Pool," dated the same day. That is the process the League's own form calls for: a council resolution, then execution by the authorized officials. A mayor signing a resolution the council adopted is the normal role. Nothing in the record suggests Proctor acted alone or outside his authority. We are requesting the minutes of the July 12, 2005 meeting to confirm the vote.

**Pooling is common practice.** The League's pools were created in the mid-1980s, when commercial insurers raised prices or stopped covering local governments. Today the League's Property and Casualty Trust serves about 440 member municipalities, and its workers' compensation pool serves 477 ([NCLM](https://www.nclm.org/news-media/southern-city/built-to-last-celebrating-municipal-insurance-pools-anniversaries/)). State law, [G.S. 58-23-5](https://ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_58/GS_58-23-5.html), expressly lets two or more local governments pool their risks, with notice to the state Insurance Commissioner.

**What's uncommon is the lawsuit.** A pool suing one of its own members, alongside the adjuster it hired, is rare. The more pointed questions are about 2024 and 2025: how the Town documented its losses, how flood zones and the dam were handled in the claim, and why its public statements about the insurance money changed.

## What we're asking, and what comes next

**Questions for the Town:**

1. How much insurance money did the Town receive for Helene, when, and for which buildings?
2. Was $1.3 million received for Town Hall, as announced in January 2025? Is the "about $100,000 paid but contested" in April 2026 about the same building?
3. What was the roughly $1.12 million moved out of the General Fund's miscellaneous revenue line between March and May 2025?
4. Has the fund demanded repayment? How much, and when?
5. Is Town Hall in a FEMA special flood hazard area? Did it carry a federal flood policy?
6. Why hasn't the Town told residents about the lawsuit?

**Questions for the League and its lawyers at Poyner Spruill:** How much is the fund seeking, and from whom? Is this the only member suit arising from Sedgwick's Helene work?

**Questions for Sedgwick:** Will it ask the court to keep the complaint sealed?
