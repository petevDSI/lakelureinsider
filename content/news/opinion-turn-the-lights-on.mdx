**There's a particular kind of small-town leader who discovers, usually around the third or fourth week of a scandal they helped create, that the actual problem was never the scandal. The actual problem was that people kept talking about it. On Wednesday, Mayor Carol Pritchett found that gear.** She told the room — in that wounded, aren't-we-better-than-this tone elected officials reach for right before they ask you to stop noticing something — that all the "duplicitous, negative" chatter about Town Hall on Facebook doesn't help Lake Lure attract visitors. "If you think that helps," she said, "you're absolutely wrong about that. You are wrong."

This is a companion piece to our news coverage of that meeting, <a href="/news/lured-market-wednesday-meeting" className="font-semibold text-(--lake) underline">Packed House, No Answers</a>, and the fuller account in <a href="/news/lured-market-wednesday-part-two" className="font-semibold text-(--lake) underline">Before the Public Spoke, the Town Did</a>. Every document behind this story is indexed at <a href="/news/lured-market-source-documents" className="font-semibold text-(--lake) underline">our running source list</a>.

Two things can be true at once. Message-board bickering is genuinely bad for a tourist town's brand, and also: that is not what is happening here.

## The math the mayor isn't doing

What's happening here is that a hurricane relief hub that fed 750 people a day when the roads were gone got a three-day eviction letter over a rent dispute the Town's own paperwork can't keep straight. What's happening is that the Town Manager told Paul Brock in writing that FEMA required the police boathouse demolished, and 48 hours later the Town's own Community Development Director told him, also in writing, that it didn't. What's happening is that the Town hasn't published a single set of council minutes since July 27 — which means the public record of how a lease got terminated is not currently a public record. What's happening is that the mayor stood at a podium the Monday before this meeting, was asked directly what the Town intends to do with the building it's fighting so hard to empty, and answered, on the record, that the Town has no intent for it at all. Then filed to evict the tenant anyway.

None of that happened on Facebook. Facebook just has a screenshot function.

## What light actually does

Here is the metaphor the mayor is groping for without quite landing on it: light and dark. Fine, let's use it properly. The mayor's theory seems to be that Lake Lure looks bad because people keep shining a light on it — so the fix is to dim the light, hand out fewer flashlights, ask everybody to please stop pointing them at the boathouse. But that's backwards, and any dental hygienist could tell her why: you don't get a cavity because the light in the exam chair is too bright. The light just tells you it's there. Turn it off and the tooth doesn't heal. It just goes on quietly rotting somewhere you can't see it anymore, which is precisely how a $4,000-a-month lease dispute over a boat shed turns into a lawsuit, a sued town, a packed hearing room, and a mayor lecturing her constituents about tone.

The brighter the light, the darker the shadows look by comparison. That's not a flaw in the light. That's the whole point of it.

And if the Town genuinely believes the story reads badly — badly enough to threaten tourism, badly enough to warrant a public scolding of its own residents — it has a remedy sitting right there, unused, gathering dust like the boathouse itself: it could turn the lights on and let people see what's actually happening. Publish the minutes. Put the termination decision to an actual recorded council vote, since as of this writing nobody can find evidence one ever happened — not the lawsuit, not the correspondence, not the termination letter itself, none of it mentions a vote. Answer Second Mountain's records request from March, the one that's been sitting unanswered since before spring turned into summer. Say, on the record, in a sentence that survives contact with a follow-up question, why an empty building is worth losing the town's most popular business over.

## What a visitor actually sees driving in

Try that last part as an exercise, because it's not hypothetical. Drive into Lake Lure the way an actual visitor does — no history with any of this, just a family looking for a weekend on the water. By this reporter's own count, four of the twelve storefronts along that beach strip are sitting empty right now. That's a third of the block gone dark — a batting average that gets you into Cooperstown and gets a downtown strip written off as dying, and it's the record before anyone touches the town's most popular tenant.

Keep driving and the rest of the tour doesn't help the mayor's case. There's an arcade with paint peeling off it in sheets, looking like it's been waiting since sometime last decade for someone to decide whether it's still open. There's a police boathouse so run-down the Town can't even agree internally on whether it needs to be condemned or leased out — that dilapidated shed is, remember, the entire spine of this fight. And if this eviction lands the way the Town's own filings suggest it's meant to, there will be one more dark window where a line used to form for barbecue and live music on a Tuesday night — the one unmistakably alive thing on that whole stretch. That's the actual tourism problem. Not a Facebook thread. A beachfront already batting .333 on empty storefronts, about to lose the one act still on stage.

If the answer to why that's worth it is a good one, it'll hold up in daylight. If it won't hold up in daylight, that's useful information too — just not for the reason the mayor thinks.

## An account already low on credibility

Instead we got the generator story. At the same meeting where residents thanked Lured Market for running a logistics hub that fed National Guard troops and FEMA crews for weeks without charging anybody a dime, the mayor offered her own Helene tale: she and her Firefly Cove neighbors shared a generator and her Starlink dish. That is a lovely, neighborly thing to do, and it is not remotely the same order of event, and reaching for it as an equivalency in the same breath as scolding people for "negative" social media posts is the kind of tone-deafness you can't manufacture on purpose. You'd have to actually believe it.

To be fair to the mayor — this is this writer's own assessment, not a documented fact — Lake Lure's credibility account has taken some real hits lately that have nothing to do with the Market at all. She herself told a room of 250 people the town suffered "no loss of life" from Helene, more than once. State autopsy records tell a different story: a Chimney Rock woman named Lisa Peeler Brady was pulled from this very lake five days after the storm, one name among the state's own confirmed Helene death toll. Her Mayor Pro Tem stood up at the August 24 meeting and said the Rocky Broad watershed had zero Helene fatalities, which the public record also disputes. The Town Manager spent a news cycle insisting a volunteer helicopter pilot had violated flight restrictions that, per the FAA, didn't exist yet at the time he was accused of violating them — a claim that helped rack up over 5,300 signatures on a petition that now names her directly. None of that is a Facebook rumor. That's the Town's own officials, on camera, getting facts wrong about who lived and who didn't.

So when the mayor says the acrimony "doesn't help our community," here's the part she's skipping: the acrimony isn't the injury. It's the bruise showing up after the injury. You don't treat a bruise by telling people to stop looking at your arm.

## Turn the lights on

Nobody organizing on Facebook wants Lake Lure to look bad. That's actually the opposite of what's happening, whatever it looks like from the dais. A town that fed itself through a hurricane, that produced a business popular enough to fill a meeting room past capacity with people begging their government to just take the rent check already, is not a town with an image problem caused by its own residents. It's a town with a transparency problem caused by the people currently lecturing those residents about tone. Everyone who lives in Lake Lure does not, in fact, have "the same vested interest" here, Madam Mayor — some of us have an interest in the boathouse, some of us have an interest in feeding our neighbors during a disaster, and a couple of people, it appears, have an interest in nobody asking too many questions about how a lease with no recorded vote turned into a three-day eviction notice against the guy who ran the shelter.

The Brocks held up a check in front of the whole town and got a shrug and a reference to procedure. That's not going to trend well no matter which platform it's on. If the goal really is protecting how Lake Lure looks to the outside world, here's a free idea, no consultant fee required: be the kind of government that looks fine in the light. It's a lot cheaper than asking everybody else to turn theirs off.

## What we're not saying

We're not asserting the mayor's "negative" comment was aimed at any specific person or post — she didn't name one, and neither do we. We're not asserting bad faith behind the Town's silence on a council vote; it's possible one happened somewhere this outlet hasn't found, and if the Town can point to it, we'll correct this piece. The vacancy count above is this reporter's own tally from a drive down the beach strip, not a Town-published figure, and storefronts change hands; readers are welcome to count for themselves.
