Lake Lure's recovery just got a major boost: FEMA has obligated three awards for the Town totaling $8,719,453.18 in federal share, and the Town says the total is nearly $9.7 million once the state cost share is counted. Rep. Tim Moore (NC-14) announced the awards on Oct. 22, 2026, as part of $13,851,577.47 in Hurricane Helene recovery funding for Lake Lure, Rutherfordton and Polk County. Lake Lure's three awards are 62.9 percent of that package.

The biggest question is the bridge. The largest bridge-related line is $2,255,390.10 in federal share ($2,505,989.00 in total, per the Town), and the Town's update says it does not currently intend to reconstruct the former pedestrian bridge. Because all three awards are fixed-cost offers under FEMA's Section 428 program, the Town says it can use the money's flexibility to address "the community's most pressing recovery, infrastructure, and resilience needs," and it will keep working with partners on a long-term multimodal connection between Lake Lure and Chimney Rock.

The other two Lake Lure awards pay for repairs across the Town's park and shoreline facilities ($4,934,881.17 federal share) and for repairs at Buffalo Creek Park, Weed Patch Mountain Trails and the dredge spoils retention ponds ($1,529,181.91). Rutherfordton receives $3,161,956.00 for two school buildings. Polk County receives $1,970,168.29 for debris removal. Moore said recovery is "far from over."

Moore, whose district includes Lake Lure, has announced a steady run of Helene awards for the Town since September 2025, including the marina docks in January and lake safety facilities in February. Of this package he said the money will "restore the Lake Lure bridge, reopen parks" and clear debris, and he thanked the administration and FEMA for prioritizing Western North Carolina. The awards themselves rest on applications the Town files with help from its FEMA consultants, and Moore's office has said it relays FEMA's approvals and defers to the Town and FEMA on how the money is used.

Two questions still matter to Lake Lure taxpayers:

- **Where will the flexible money go?** The Town says some damaged recreational assets may be restored or replaced and other funding may go to "alternative critical infrastructure." It has not named the projects.
- **When does the money arrive?** An award is an obligation, not a deposit. The Town's own June 2026 figures show how far those two lag.

## Lake Lure's $8.72 million

The largest Lake Lure award, $4,934,881.17, covers repairs at multiple park facilities. The release lists Morse Park, Lake Lure Beach and Water Park, lake access ramps, the lake seawall and cobblestone walkway, Pool Creek Picnic Park, Lake Lure green space, a fire department dry hydrant and the Washburn Marina seawall.

A separate $1,529,181.91 returns Buffalo Creek Park, Weed Patch Mountain Trails and the dredge spoils retention ponds to their pre-disaster design, function and capacity, in the release's words.

**Morse Park.** The Town has worked on the park in stages since the storm. The east end held a soft opening in April 2025. The Town's Feb. 10, 2026 meeting summary reports the Welcome Center building was removed because it could not be salvaged. By January 2026 the Town planned riprap along the Morse Park shoreline and mechanical dredging near the park. In May 2026 it was seeking quotes to grade the remaining sediment. The Town's September 2026 news summary says that once park restoration and the Comprehensive Plan are complete, the Town can begin evaluating long-term improvements and development in the Morse Park area. This award is the first large FEMA figure tied to that work in the releases reviewed.

**Washburn Marina.** The new award covers the marina seawall. It is separate from the $1,796,864.36 FEMA award announced in January 2026 for the marina docks, which carried a $1,996,515.95 total project cost at a 90 percent federal share. The Town reopened the marina in August 2026.

The release does not itemize dollars by facility. How the $4.93 million splits among eight named facilities is not public in what has been released.

## The bridge: a second award, a different section

The $2,255,390.10 award is the second FEMA announcement tied to the span between Lake Lure and Chimney Rock, and the two do not match.

1. **September 2025.** Moore announced $2,565,168.30 under Section 406 to replace the Lake Lure Flowering Bridge, on a $2,850,187.00 project at a 90 percent federal share. FEMA's own Sept. 16, 2025 list named the same project.
2. **Two days later.** The Town said it had not confirmed any plan to use those funds for the bridge. It said it was working with FEMA on a fixed-cost offer to redirect the money to critical infrastructure, and that replacement was cost prohibitive. It also said Helene had reshaped the shoreline and nearly doubled the span a new bridge would need to cross. Moore's office said it relayed what FEMA had approved and deferred to the Town and FEMA on any alternate use.
3. **Late September 2025.** FOX Carolina reported that the grant would go only toward a pedestrian walkway, with the Flowering Bridge organization raising money separately for the gardens and Education Center. That account sits uneasily with the Town's statement, and the records reviewed do not resolve it.
4. **October 2026.** FEMA now lists $2,255,390.10 in federal share under Section 428 for the pedestrian bridge between Lake Lure and Chimney Rock, and Moore's release says it will help "restore the Lake Lure bridge." The Town's update puts the total at $2,505,989.00, "in addition to previously allocated funds," and says it "does not currently intend to reconstruct the former pedestrian bridge."

The Town's update answers part of this. It confirms that all three Lake Lure obligations are approved fixed-cost offers under Section 428, which is the path the Town said in 2025 it was pursuing. It describes the bridge-related $2,505,989.00 as "in addition to previously allocated funds," which suggests this award is not simply a replacement for the 2025 grant. The Town does not give the earlier total or say how the two fit together, so the combined amount tied to the former bridge is not yet public.

On what the money will do, the Town is direct about the bridge and general about everything else. It "does not currently intend to reconstruct the former pedestrian bridge." While "certain damaged recreational assets may ultimately be restored or replaced," other funding "may be directed toward alternative critical infrastructure and recovery needs that provide broader community benefit." Separately, the Town will keep working with local, regional and state partners on a long-term multimodal connection between Lake Lure and Chimney Rock as permanent transportation improvements in the area move forward. The Town has not named which projects will receive the redirected money.

## Rutherfordton schools and Polk County debris

Rutherfordton's $3,161,956.00 falls under Section 406, the permanent repair track, and supports restoring two school buildings at 132 and 134 Maple Street. The release does not name the school or its owner.

Polk County's $1,970,168.29 falls under Section 407 and supports Helene debris removal. Polk has appeared in several recent announcements. A June 10, 2026 release listed $172,895.07 to reimburse the county for administering Public Assistance grants. A February 2026 release listed $4,146,998.35 to the N.C. Department of Transportation for permanent repairs to seven segments of Pearson Falls Road in Polk County.

## Sections 406, 407 and 428, explained

The section number tells you how the money is calculated, and who carries the risk if costs run over.

- **Section 406** funds permanent repair or replacement of damaged public facilities. It is the standard track.
- **Section 407** is the section Moore's release ties to Polk County's debris removal.
- **Section 428** is the Public Assistance Alternative Procedures. FEMA, the state and the applicant agree on a fixed-cost estimate for a project, and the grant is made on that estimate.

Congress added Section 428 through the Sandy Recovery Improvement Act of 2013. FEMA's stated goals for it were lower federal costs and more flexibility in administering aid.

For a small town, three features of the fixed-cost track matter:

1. **Flexibility.** An applicant that accepts a fixed-cost offer is generally not required to rebuild a facility exactly as it stood before the disaster. This is why a bridge award can be redirected.
2. **Overrun risk.** The applicant accepts responsibility for costs above the estimate.
3. **Savings.** Cost savings can be used for activities that reduce risk in future disasters, within program rules.

The release describes the Buffalo Creek, Weed Patch and retention pond work as restoring facilities to pre-disaster design. That award is on the fixed-cost track too, so the Town would carry any overrun on it.

## Obligated is not paid

FEMA announces obligations. The Town is reimbursed after it spends the money and submits documentation, so cash arrives well after the headline.

The Town's own numbers show the lag:

- On Feb. 10, 2026, the Town reported 21 projects obligated and $4.2 million received on 12 of them.
- In June 2026, the mayor reported more than $5.15 million in FEMA reimbursements to date. Still obligated but unpaid were the marina docks ($1,996,515.95 project cost), lake safety facilities ($1,482,977.10) and town structures ($31,939.35).
- Carolina Public Press reported on June 29, 2026 that the Town had not yet received the funds announced in February.
- The Town spent $2.5 million in the first month after Helene, about half its annual revenue, from its general fund, then applied for reimbursement at the end of October 2024.

**The local share.** Moore's releases on the Flowering Bridge, marina docks and lake safety facilities each listed a 90 percent federal share. This release lists none. The Town's update says its larger figures include the state cost share, but it does not say whether the Town itself owes any portion of these awards, so the Town's local share is not yet public.

**The federal posture.** In June 2025 Reuters reported that FEMA had denied North Carolina's request to extend full-cost reimbursement past an initial 180-day period, and that the governor warned of about $200 million in added state costs.

**Scale.** The Town's other recovery needs dwarf this package. Reuters reported projections of $35 million to move the wastewater plant and up to $100 million to replace the lakebed sewer system. Carolina Journal reported dam replacement estimates reaching $174 million. The $8.72 million for Lake Lure is about 5 percent of the dam estimate alone.

## Where recovery stands

Helene struck on Sept. 27, 2024. The Broad River surge sent debris and sediment into Lake Lure, and the area took about 22.5 inches of rain over Sept. 24 to 28, per FOX Weather. The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers removed roughly 1.2 million tons of sediment and more than 100,000 cubic yards of debris, which FEMA paid for directly. Governor Stein joined the Town at Morse Park in May 2026 to celebrate the lake's reopening, and the Town scheduled the beach to reopen May 23.
