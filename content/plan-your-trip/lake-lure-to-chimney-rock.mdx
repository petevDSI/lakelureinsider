<PageHero subhead="The two are 8 miles apart — here's how to connect them into one day" />

<QuickAnswer />

The drive from Lake Lure to Chimney Rock State Park takes <Fact id="drive.lake-lure-to-chimney-rock.minutes" /> — <Fact id="drive.lake-lure-to-chimney-rock.miles" /> along US-64 through Hickory Nut Gorge. Lake Lure sits at the western end of the gorge; Chimney Rock State Park is at the eastern end. They share the same valley, and the drive between them is the gorge itself — scenic, two-lane mountain road with the Rocky Broad River running alongside it.

This is not a "side trip." These two are the reason people come to this part of western NC, and doing both in one day is the standard plan.

## The Drive

Start at Lake Lure town center and take US-64 East. No turns. The road follows the Rocky Broad River through the gorge for about 8 miles. Just before the park entrance, you pass through Chimney Rock Village — a half-mile of restaurants, outfitter shops, and galleries. The park entrance is immediately east of the village.

On the way back, NC-9 from Lake Lure toward Black Mountain is an alternate route that avoids US-64. It's longer but passes through different terrain. Most people take US-64 both ways; it's the more direct road.

Check [drivenc.gov](https://drivenc.gov/) before a winter or post-rain drive. Both roads are mountain roads and can be affected by rockfall or flooding after heavy weather.

## How to Split the Day

**Lake Lure in the morning, Chimney Rock in the afternoon.**

The lake is best early: calmer water for kayaking and paddleboarding, the beach before the crowds build, and boat rental availability before the weekend rush. Plan to be on the water by 9 AM.

Drive to Chimney Rock by 1–2 PM. The entry cutoff is 5:30 PM in main season, but the park closes at 7:00 PM — arriving at 2 PM gives you five hours. Skyline Trail is the most popular route and takes 1–2 hours depending on pace. Hickory Nut Falls Trail is easier and takes about an hour round trip.

If you arrive at Chimney Rock at 4 PM, skip Skyline (it closes at 5:00 PM) and do Hickory Nut Falls instead — it's open until 6:00 PM and the falls are worth seeing.

<InsiderTip>
The gorge has two distinct personalities. Lake Lure is flat, open water with mountain ridges framing it — it reads as "resort lake." Chimney Rock is vertical — the rock formation towers over you and the gorge is narrow. They're 8 miles apart and feel like completely different landscapes. That contrast is the reason the combination works as well as it does.
</InsiderTip>

## If You're Staying Multiple Days

Splitting Lake Lure and Chimney Rock across two days gives you the most time at each. Do the lake for a full day — boat rental, beach, and one of the guided tours — then spend the full next day at Chimney Rock with time for both major trails and the summit.

The cabins and vacation rentals between the two (in the gorge and up the surrounding ridges) are positioned to make both easy. You wake up and you're 15 minutes from everything.
