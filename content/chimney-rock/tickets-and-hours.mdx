## Hours

<ChimneyRockHours />

<InsiderTip>
The gate closes at 5:30 PM (main season) for new entries — but the park stays open until 7:00 PM. That's 90 minutes of buffer. Arriving at 5:15 PM still gets you a full Hickory Nut Falls hike and the elevator ride to the summit. Late afternoon in the gorge also has better light than midday. This is consistently one of the best times to visit, and most people don't try it because they assume the 5:30 PM sign means the park closes at 5:30 PM.
</InsiderTip>

<InsiderTip>
Skyline Trail closes at 5:00 PM — 30 minutes before the entry deadline. If Skyline is your reason for coming, your real cutoff is closer to 4:30 PM. After you park and walk to the trailhead, you'll have marginal time if you arrive at 5:00 PM and rangers will turn you back. Hickory Nut Falls Trail closes at 6:00 PM and is the better late-afternoon option regardless — the falls are more dramatic than the view from Skyline for most first-time visitors, and there's less elevation gain.
</InsiderTip>

## What Your Ticket Covers

Your day ticket gets you into everything the park contains for the day: the elevator to the summit level, the Rock itself, all trails including Skyline and Hickory Nut Falls, and all overlooks. There's no secondary admission charge once you're inside.

The elevator is not an upsell — it's included. It goes from the lower visitor area directly to the summit level in seconds and is fully accessible. Most people take it up and use the staircase on the way back down.

What isn't included: food and drinks at the Sky Lounge Gift Shop & Deli, and anything in the gift stores. Annual passholders get 15% off both.

## When to Buy in Advance

<AffiliateCTA affiliateId="getyourguide-chimney-rock" headline="Book Chimney Rock Online" subtext="Skip the ticket window line on busy fall weekends — arrival-window reservations available on GetYourGuide." />

Reservations are time-slotted into one-hour arrival windows. They're not required, and if you miss your window, you can still walk up anytime the gate is open.

Buy tickets in advance for: Memorial Day weekend, the Fourth of July, Labor Day weekend, every weekend in July, and any weekend from mid-October through early November. Fall foliage turns the gorge orange and gold; those are the most visited weeks of the year. Peak-fall Saturdays can see the main lot full before 10 AM.

Skip the advance ticket for: weekday visits in any season, and most weekends outside of July and October. Walking up is fine.

Annual passholders never need reservations.

<SectionHeader kicker="Know before you go">Getting In: Parking and Arrival</SectionHeader>

The main lot sits at the base of the rock, a short walk from the ticket plaza. On fall weekends, the main lot fills fast — plan on parking farther down and taking the shuttle, or arrive early enough to beat it. The shuttle wait is short.

Once through the ticket plaza, you reach the elevator or the staircase. Both go to the same place. The elevator takes seconds; the stairs take a minute or two and serve as a decent warm-up. Park staff are straightforward about directing you.

Road access: US-64 from Hendersonville and NC-9 from Lake Lure are both open. Check [drivenc.gov](https://drivenc.gov/) for current conditions before a winter trip or after heavy rain — the gorge roads are mountain roads.

<SectionHeader kicker="Annual passes">Is a Pass Worth It?</SectionHeader>

The math is simpler than it looks.

An adult day ticket costs $17. An adult annual pass costs $32. The pass pays for itself on your second visit — two day tickets would cost $34, two dollars more than the pass.

Youth works the same way: $8 day ticket, $14 annual pass. Break-even at the second visit.

The **30-day upgrade window** is the detail most people don't know: you can buy a day ticket and upgrade to an annual pass within 30 days of your visit, with your original receipt, at the Ticket Plaza. The upgrade costs $15 for adults and $6 for youth. That's exactly the difference between the day and annual pass prices — so upgrading later costs the same as buying the pass upfront.

<InsiderTip>
For individuals and couples, you cannot make a wrong decision at the ticket window. Buy the day ticket. If you liked the park enough to return, bring your receipt and upgrade within 30 days. You pay the same either way — the math comes out to $32 whether you buy the annual pass upfront or pay $17 now and $15 later.

The one exception is families. There is no published family upgrade rate. Upgrading five individual tickets after the fact costs $30 (2 adults) + $18 (3 youth) = $48 on top of the $58 you paid in day tickets, totaling $106 — that's $26 more than the $80 family pass bought up front. If you're bringing two adults and two or more youth, buy the family pass at the window from the start.
</InsiderTip>

## Annual Pass Benefits Beyond Admission

The pass includes more than free entry:

- Unlimited visits for 12 months from your purchase month
- $2 off each adult and $1 off each child ticket for friends you bring in
- 15% off at Cliff Dwellers Gifts and the Sky Lounge Gift Shop & Deli
- Access to passholder-only events and discounts on guided hikes and workshops
- Partner discounts at other area attractions (published annually as a PDF on the park website)
- No reservations required, ever
