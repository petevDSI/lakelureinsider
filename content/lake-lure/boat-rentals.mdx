<SectionHeader kicker="Before you book">The three questions that change the price</SectionHeader>

Published rates are the starting point, not the total. Three things move the final number and none of them are usually on the website.

**Is fuel included?** On pontoons, often not. Across a full day this is the difference between the advertised rate and a noticeably larger bill. Ask for the answer in writing when you reserve.

**What is the deposit, and when is it released?** Most operators hold a card. Amounts and release timing vary, and a hold can tie up part of your vacation budget for days after you get home.

**What is the cancellation window?** Weather in the gorge turns fast. An operator who lets you cancel the morning of is worth paying a little more for in shoulder season, when one storm can take out your only boating day.

<InsiderTip>
**The half-day break-even is about <Fact id="boat-rental.half-day-breakeven" />.** Using Lake Lure Adventure Company's published rates for their 20 ft boat, paying by the hour is cheaper right up until that point — then the half-day package wins. The full day costs less than two half days, so anything past roughly <Fact id="boat-rental.full-day-breakeven" /> should be booked as a full day rather than stacked.

The practical version: a quick spin, pay hourly. A real afternoon, book the half day. Bringing a cooler and planning to swim, book the full day — you will use it, and the effective hourly cost drops by about a fifth.
</InsiderTip>

<SectionHeader kicker="Local operators vs. marketplaces">Where you book matters</SectionHeader>

The table separates local operators from peer-to-peer marketplaces on purpose, because they are genuinely different products.

**Local operators** run a fleet from a fixed location on the lake. You get an orientation, a phone number that answers if something goes wrong on the water, and a boat maintained by someone whose business depends on it. Prices are higher and less negotiable.

**Peer-to-peer platforms** list individual owners. Rates start much lower, but you are renting one specific boat from one specific person. Condition, pickup location, and flexibility vary enormously between listings. Read the reviews on the individual boat, not on the platform.

For a first visit with family aboard, the local operator is usually the right call. For an experienced boater on a budget who can evaluate a listing, the marketplace can be a real bargain.

<SectionHeader kicker="Know before you go">Capacity is a rating, not a recommendation</SectionHeader>

A pontoon rated for 13 passengers holds 13 adults the way a car rated for five seats five adults — technically true, not pleasant for a full day. If your group is at the top of a boat's rating and you plan to be out for hours with coolers, chairs, and towels, size up.

The other thing nobody mentions: pontoons are slow. That is the point — they are floating porches. If your mental image involves covering the whole lake and hitting several stops, budget more time than you think, or ask about a faster hull.

<SectionHeader kicker="Timing">When to book, and when not to bother</SectionHeader>

**Book two to three weeks ahead** for any Saturday or Sunday between Memorial Day and Labor Day, and for every weekend in October. Fall foliage in the gorge is the busiest stretch of the year, and boats sell out before lodging does.

**Book a few days ahead** for summer weekdays.

**Walk up** in April, May, and November, when you can usually get a boat the same morning and rates are softest.

Morning is the better half of the day on Lake Lure. The water is typically calmest before noon, the light is better against the gorge walls, and summer thunderstorms tend to build in the afternoon.
