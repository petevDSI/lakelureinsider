If you're driving an EV to Lake Lure or Chimney Rock, plan your charging before you arrive — there's no public charger at either one. That's not unusual for a small mountain town, but it catches people off guard who assume a tourist destination this size will have something. It doesn't, not yet.

The good news: real options exist within about a half hour, and one of them — Asheville — is a place you might already be passing through or stopping in anyway.

## Which One Should You Use

If you're just topping off enough to get home and the wait doesn't matter, Hendersonville's Dogwood Parking Lot is the easiest call — free, reliable ChargePoint hardware, and you're a short walk from Main Street's shops and restaurants while you wait. Black Mountain is marginally closer but runs on a smaller network with no live status reporting, so treat it as a backup rather than a destination.

If you actually need a meaningful charge in a reasonable amount of time, skip the Level 2 options entirely and go to Asheville. Tesla drivers (and NACS/CCS-adapter-equipped EVs) have the 8-stall Supercharger on South Tunnel Road. Everyone else has Asheville Ford's DC fast chargers — 4 CCS ports, public access, no purchase required. Either one turns a "top off" into an actual meaningful charge in well under an hour, and both put you near enough to downtown Asheville to make the drive worth it on its own.

<InsiderTip>
None of these are inside Lake Lure or Chimney Rock — they're all a 20–30 minute drive away. If your plan is "charge when I get low," rework it. Charge before you leave home or your hotel, and treat the drive out here as one-way range you need to have covered both directions.
</InsiderTip>
