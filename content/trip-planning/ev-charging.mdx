If you're driving an EV to Lake Lure, the picture has changed: the Town installed a public charger at Morse Park in June 2026, right next to the Welcome Center and Town Hall. Chimney Rock State Park itself, a few miles up US-64/74A, still doesn't have one — so if your day is centered on the park rather than the lake, don't count on topping off there.

Either way, it's worth knowing what's actually on offer before you arrive. Lake Lure's station is small (two ports) and paid, not free, and real backup options exist within about a half hour if you need them — including one, Asheville, you might already be passing through.

## Charging in Lake Lure

Morse Park's Welcome Center parking lot has two Level 2 J1772 ports, run by Nova EV Charging through the ChargeUP Driver app — download the app, plug in, scan the QR code at the charger, and pick a pricing option before it starts charging. It's open 24 hours, but it isn't free like the Town's original 2018 Duke Energy-funded station at the same spot was; expect to pay through the app, with no flat rate published anywhere we could find.

At Chimney Rock State Park itself, there is no charger on-site as of <Fact id="chimney-rock.ev-charging.none-found" />.

## Which One Should You Use

If you just need a top-off and don't mind paying through an app, Morse Park is the obvious choice — it's in town, open 24 hours, and doesn't cost you a drive. Just don't count on it exclusively: it's only two ports, and the Town has had to bring the station back online before after a period of downtime.

If you want something free instead, or Morse Park's two ports are occupied, Hendersonville's Dogwood Parking Lot is the easiest call — free, reliable ChargePoint hardware, and a short walk from Main Street's shops and restaurants while you wait. Black Mountain is marginally closer but runs on a smaller network with no live status reporting, so treat it as a backup rather than a destination.

If you actually need a meaningful charge in a reasonable amount of time, skip the Level 2 options entirely and go to Asheville. Tesla drivers (and NACS/CCS-adapter-equipped EVs) have the 8-stall Supercharger on South Tunnel Road. Everyone else has Asheville Ford's DC fast chargers — 4 CCS ports, public access, no purchase required. Either one turns a "top off" into an actual meaningful charge in well under an hour, and both put you near enough to downtown Asheville to make the drive worth it on its own.

<InsiderTip>
Download the ChargeUP Driver app before you arrive, not after you've plugged in and are standing in the Morse Park lot with no signal to spare. And since it's only two ports with a documented history of going down, treat it as a nice-to-have rather than your charging plan — especially if you're headed on to Chimney Rock State Park afterward, which still has nothing on-site.
</InsiderTip>
